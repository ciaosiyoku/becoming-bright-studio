# Testing with a child

A checklist can't tell you whether a lesson teaches. A 6-year-old can. Do this before adding any reward game, map
or new skill, and again after changing a teach card or the feedback.

**Status:** not done yet. The app has passed its automated tests and has been clicked through in a browser, but
no child has used it. Record the first session below.

## Before you start

- One child, about 6, who hasn't used the app. Ask a parent's permission if it isn't your own child.
- One skill only. Start with **Add within 5** (the paper demo in [LEARNING-CONTRACT.md](LEARNING-CONTRACT.md)).
- A tablet with the sound on, and paper for notes. Sit beside the child, not opposite.
- Don't help, hint or praise during the questions. Say only "Do what you think" if they look at you.

## The session (about 10 minutes)

1. **Teach card.** Let the card read itself aloud. Then cover the screen and ask: *"What do you do to add?"*
   - **Pass:** they say the rule in their own words ("start at the number and jump"), or show it with their finger.
   - **Fail:** they can't, or they repeat the example's numbers. **The card is wrong.** Change its picture or
     sentence before testing anything else.
2. **Guided tries and checks.** Watch the moment after each answer.
   - **Pass:** after a red answer they look at the green one or the number line, or wait for the voice.
   - **Fail:** they tap "Next" (or anything) straight away, or tap at random to make the screen move.
     **The feedback is wrong:** red is being read as "go faster". Make the reason more visible, or say it first.
3. **After the result,** ask: *"What was hard?"* and *"Do you want to do another one?"* Write down their words.

## What to change, by what you saw

| You saw | Change |
|---|---|
| Can't say the rule after the teach card | The teach card: simpler sentence, one picture, slower voice |
| Taps "Next" without looking | The feedback: the reason read first, the number line bigger, or "Next" waiting longer |
| Taps choices at random from the start | The question is too hard: test the skill before it |
| Bored by check 3 | Fewer guided tries, or a shorter session (but keep 5 checks) |
| Gets 5 of 5 without effort | The skill is too easy for this child: move them on, and note it |
